Tax Brackets 2025: Key Dates, Holidays & Full Overview

Official IRS 2025 tax brackets: Revenue Procedure 2024-40 marginal rates, standard deductions, capital gains thresholds, and key 2025-2026 deadlines.

Official IRS Revenue Procedure 2024-40: The 2025 Tax Landscape

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) officially established the federal income tax brackets, standard deduction amounts, and statutory inflation parameters for Tax Year 2025 (filed in early 2026) under Revenue Procedure 2024-40. Federal income tax continues to be assessed across seven progressive marginal tax brackets: 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%. Income thresholds across all brackets were adjusted upward by approximately 2.8% to 3.0% to reflect cost-of-living increases and prevent 'bracket creep' for American workers.

Tax year 2025 represents a pivotal financial milestone: it marks the final operational year of the individual tax cuts enacted under the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA). Understanding your exact marginal bracket, anticipating your eventual tax refund status, optimizing after-tax retirement contributions through a Roth IRA, and planning around statutory filing deadlines are crucial for preserving household wealth.

Standard Deductions for Tax Year 2025

The standard deduction reduces your Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) before your remaining earnings are subjected to marginal brackets. For 2025, standard deduction amounts rose across all categories:

  • Married Filing Jointly / Surviving Spouses: $30,000 (an increase of $800 from $29,200 in 2024).
  • Head of Household: $22,500 (an increase of $600 from $21,900 in 2024).
  • Single Filers: $15,000 (an increase of $400 from $14,600 in 2024).
  • Married Filing Separately: $15,000 (an increase of $400 from $14,600 in 2024).

Additional Standard Deduction for Seniors (Age 65+) and Blind Filers

Taxpayers who reach age 65 before the close of the tax year or who are legally blind qualify for an extra statutory deduction. For 2025, the additional amount is $1,600 per qualified individual for married filers (or $3,200 if both spouses qualify), and $2,000 for unmarried single filers or heads of household.

Long-Term Capital Gains Tax Brackets 2025

Profits from the sale of capital assets (including stocks, index funds, real estate, and crypto assets) held longer than 365 days enjoy preferential long-term capital gains tax rates of 0%, 15%, or 20%. The statutory 2025 thresholds are:

0% Rate Up to $48,350 Up to $96,700 Up to $64,750
15% Rate $48,351 to $533,400 $96,701 to $600,050 $64,751 to $566,700
20% Rate Over $533,400 Over $600,050 Over $566,700

Note on Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT): High-earning taxpayers with Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) exceeding $200,000 for single filers or $250,000 for married couples filing jointly are also subject to an additional 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax assessed on investment earnings, effectively raising the top capital gains rate to 23.8%.

Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT), Credits & Savings Limits for 2025

Revenue Procedure 2024-40 also adjusted major parallel tax systems and contribution thresholds:

1. Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) Exemptions

The AMT prevents high earners from utilizing excessive tax deductions to avoid liability. For 2025, AMT exemption amounts are:

  • Single Filers: $88,100 (phaseout begins at $626,350).
  • Married Filing Jointly: $137,000 (phaseout begins at $1,252,700).

2. Key Tax Credits for Families

  • Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC): The maximum EITC for low-to-moderate-income families with three or more qualifying children increases to $8,046 (up from $7,830 in 2024).
  • Child Tax Credit: Remains at $2,000 per qualifying child under age 17, with the refundable 'Additional Child Tax Credit' portion capped at $1,700.

3. Retirement & Health Savings Account Limits for 2025

  • 401(k), 403(b), and Thrift Savings Plan Limits: Employee salary deferral limit is $23,500. Standard catch-up for age 50+ is $7,500 (total $31,000). Under SECURE 2.0 rules, a special enhanced catch-up limit of $11,250 applies to workers aged 60, 61, 62, and 63 (total $34,750).
  • Traditional and Roth IRAs: Contribution limit is $7,000, with an additional $1,000 catch-up for individuals age 50 and older ($8,000 total).
  • Health Savings Accounts (HSA): Contribution limit is $4,300 for self-only coverage and $8,550 for family coverage, with a $1,000 catch-up for age 55+.

Official 2025-2026 IRS Tax Calendar Deadlines & Holiday Adjustments

Taxpayers must align their estimated payments and return filings with official IRS statutory deadlines. When a statutory due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or official federal holiday (such as federal statutory closures observed on Memorial Day), the deadline legally shifts to the next immediate business day:

Impact of District of Columbia and State Holidays on Filing Deadlines

Under IRC Section 7503, legal holidays recognized in the District of Columbia impact federal tax deadlines nationwide. Emancipation Day (April 16 in Washington D.C.) can push national filing deadlines to April 17 or 18 when April 15 falls on a weekend or concurrent holiday. Furthermore, taxpayers residing in Massachusetts and Maine frequently receive an additional 24-hour extension to April 16 or 17 due to the regional celebration of Patriots' Day on the third Monday of April.

The Impending 2025-2026 TCJA Sunset Cliff: Proactive Planning

Tax year 2025 is the final tax year governed by the individual tax reforms of the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA). Without congressional legislative extension, the following statutory reversions will automatically take effect on January 1, 2026:

  • Marginal Rates Revert: The top 37% bracket will revert to 39.6%. The 12% bracket reverts to 15%, 22% reverts to 25%, and 24% reverts to 28%.
  • Standard Deduction Halved: The elevated standard deduction will be reduced by roughly 50% (adjusted for post-2017 inflation), forcing tens of millions of middle-income households back into itemizing on Schedule A.
  • SALT Deduction Cap Eliminates: The controversial $10,000 ceiling on State and Local Tax (SALT) itemized deductions expires, allowing high-tax state residents (CA, NY, NJ) to deduct unlimited state taxes again.
  • Child Tax Credit Slashed: The credit drops from $2,000 per child back to $1,000 per child, with significantly lower income phaseout thresholds.
  • Estate & Gift Tax Exemption Drops: The lifetime estate exemption—which reaches approximately $13.99 million per individual ($27.98 million per married couple) in 2025—will plunge by roughly half to approximately $7 million per person.

Strategic Steps to Take Before December 31, 2025

  1. Accelerate Ordinary Income: High earners should consider realizing income in 2025 while the 24%, 32%, and 37% brackets remain locked in, rather than deferring into 2026 when higher rates take effect.
  2. Execute Strategic Roth Conversions: Transfer funds from Traditional pre-tax IRAs into Roth accounts at today's known lower rates before brackets compress in 2026.
  3. Harvest Capital Gains: Lock in capital gains under the current 15% and 20% thresholds before potential tax policy modifications.
  4. Review Estate Plans: High-net-worth families should utilize their historically high $13.99 million lifetime gift tax exemption before the statutory sunset reduces it by 50%.
