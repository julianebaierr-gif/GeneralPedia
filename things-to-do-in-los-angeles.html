Things to Do in Los Angeles: Top Attractions Guide

Discover the best things to do in Los Angeles. Explore iconic sights, beaches, movie studios, free museums, and smart 2026 itineraries.

Amir Hassan
Recent
18 min read
Travel & Itinerary Notice: Operating hours, exhibition schedules, ticket prices, and entry reservation policies for Los Angeles attractions change seasonally. GeneralPedia verifies destination information regularly; visitors are encouraged to confirm timed entry passes and parking rules on official venue portals before arrival.

Planning the ultimate list of things to do in los angeles can feel exhilarating yet overwhelming. Spanning more than 500 square miles of sun-soaked coastal plains, rugged mountain canyons, and distinct cultural enclaves, the City of Angels is not a single walkable downtown. Instead, Los Angeles functions as a sprawling collection of unique urban villages, each offering its own culinary traditions, architectural treasures, cinematic heritage, and community spirit.

From the cinematic history of Hollywood backlots and the sweeping skyline panoramas of Griffith Observatory to the bohemian skate culture of Venice Beach and world-class collections at The Getty Center, Los Angeles provides an unmatched range of travel experiences. This comprehensive 2026 visitor guide covers must-see landmarks, free cultural attractions, studio tours, coastal gems, authentic culinary corridors, hidden local treasures, and a smart regional itinerary designed to maximize your time while avoiding notorious freeway traffic.

Iconic Landmarks and Panoramic Skyline Views

First-time visitors heading to Southern California often picture the gleaming white letters of Mount Lee and golden Pacific sunsets. Starting your journey with these classic viewpoints establishes a true sense of scale across the metropolitan basin:

1. Griffith Observatory and Griffith Park

Perched high on the south-facing slope of Mount Hollywood, Griffith Observatory is arguably the most beloved public monument in Southern California. Completed in 1935 through a civic bequest by Griffith J. Griffith, the Beaux-Arts facility offers free admission to its expansive terraces, copper-domed public telescopes, and interactive astronomy galleries.

  • Viewing Highlights: Inspecting sunspots through the historic coelostat solar telescope, gazing through the 12-inch Zeiss refracting telescope after dark, and attending immersive digital sky shows inside the Samuel Oschin Planetarium.
  • Visiting Strategy: Arrive in the late afternoon to secure public parking, explore the science exhibits, watch the golden hour sunset paint the downtown skyscrapers in warm amber tones, and watch the city transform into an endless grid of twinkling evening lights.

2. The Hollywood Sign

Erected in 1923 as an advertisement for the upscale real estate development Hollywoodland, the 45-foot-tall capital letters atop Mount Lee have stood as the premier international symbol of cinematic ambition for over a century. While driving directly to the letters is prohibited to protect residential neighborhoods, several vantage points provide picture-perfect photo angles:

  • Lake Hollywood Park: Offers an open grassy lawn with direct, unobstructed views of the sign rising directly above the palm trees.
  • Innsdale Trail & Brush Canyon Trail: Safe, scenic, and pedestrian-friendly hiking trails through the chaparral hills of Griffith Park leading toward the ridge behind Mount Lee.
  • Griffith Observatory Terrace: A comfortable, elevated long-range photo spot that captures both the historic letters and the surrounding canyon hillsides.

3. Hollywood Walk of Fame and TCL Chinese Theatre

Stretching along Hollywood Boulevard and Vine Street, the Walk of Fame features more than 2,700 terrazzo-and-brass stars commemorating legends of film, television, audio recording, radio, and live performance. Just outside the historic TCL Chinese Theatre (opened in 1927 by showman Sid Grauman), visitors can step directly into the concrete handprints and footprints of legendary actors ranging from classic Golden Age stars to modern icons.

4. The Hollywood Bowl

Nestled inside a natural canyon amphitheater, the Hollywood Bowl has served as Southern California's premier outdoor music venue since 1922. During the summer season, visitors can attend performances by the Los Angeles Philharmonic, film score retrospectives, and headlining concerts. A cherished local tradition allows concertgoers to bring their own picnic dinners and wine into the venue for select classical and jazz performances.

Movie Magic: Legendary Hollywood Studio Tours

As the historical birthplace of modern cinematic storytelling, Los Angeles allows visitors to step behind the camera and explore real, functioning production lots:

Warner Bros. Studio Tour Hollywood (Burbank)

Located over the Cahuenga Pass in Burbank, the Warner Bros. Studio Tour provides an intimate, authentic look at one of the busiest working studio lots in the entertainment industry. Guided electric carts transport visitors through outdoor backlot streets that have doubled for New York, Chicago, and small-town America across thousands of classic television programs and films.

Tour highlights include walking through Stage 48: Script to Screen, exploring genuine prop archives from the DC Universe and Harry Potter franchises, and sitting on the actual orange couch inside the recreated Central Perk set from Friends.

Universal Studios Hollywood

Combining high-octane theme park thrills with historical movie magic, Universal Studios Hollywood sits on a hillside overlooking the San Fernando Valley. The cornerstone of the destination is the world-famous Studio Tour, a 60-minute narrated tram ride through working soundstages, the Bates Motel from Psycho, the crashing jet set from War of the Worlds, and immersive 3D simulations.

Beyond the studio tram, the park features themed lands such as The Wizarding World of Harry Potter and Super Nintendo World, where visitors interact with full-scale video game environments using digital Power-Up Bands.

Paramount Pictures Studio Tour

For pure historic Hollywood nostalgia, Paramount Pictures is the only major working motion picture studio still physically located in the heart of historic Hollywood. Guided two-hour walking tours wind past the iconic Melrose Gate, the historic soundstages where classic cinema was filmed, and the expansive 5-acre New York Street backlot.

World-Class Art, Museums, and Architecture

Beyond entertainment, Los Angeles boasts an extraordinary density of cultural institutions that rival London, Paris, and New York:

The Getty Center (Brentwood)

Designed by architect Richard Meier, The Getty Center sits atop a scenic ridge in the Santa Monica Mountains. The museum campus features Italian travertine stone buildings, tranquil cactus gardens, water fountains, and panoramic vistas stretching from downtown skyscrapers to the Pacific Ocean.

The permanent collection showcases European paintings, drawings, sculpture, and decorative arts from the Middle Ages to the early twentieth century. Notable masterpieces include Vincent van Gogh's Irises, Rembrandt's An Old Man in Military Costume, and Claude Monet's Wheatstacks. While admission to the galleries and grounds is free, visitors must reserve a timed-entry pass online in advance.

Los Angeles County Museum of Art (LACMA) and Museum Row

Situated along the Miracle Mile, LACMA is the largest art museum in the western United States. Outside the museum entrance stands Urban Light, Chris Burden's iconic public art installation composed of 202 restored cast-iron antique street lamps from the 1920s and 1930s.

Directly adjacent to LACMA lies the La Brea Tar Pits, an active urban paleontological excavation site where natural asphalt has seeped from the earth for over 50,000 years. Inside the museum, visitors can view prehistoric fossils of saber-toothed cats, dire wolves, and Columbian mammoths excavated directly from the surrounding park grounds.

The Academy Museum of Motion Pictures

Located right next door on Wilshire Boulevard, the Academy Museum celebrates the art, science, and history of moviemaking. Designed by Renzo Piano, the museum features four floors of cinematic exhibits, including original props like the Ruby Slippers from The Wizard of Oz, authentic Oscar statuettes, Hayao Miyazaki animation retrospectives, and the breathtaking glass-domed Dolby Family Terrace overlooking the Hollywood Hills.

The Broad and Downtown LA Arts

In the heart of Grand Avenue in Downtown Los Angeles, The Broad presents one of the most prominent collections of postwar and contemporary art in the world. Housed in an innovative honeycomb-like building designed by Diller Scofidio + Renfro, the museum features works by Jean-Michel Basquiat, Andy Warhol, Cindy Sherman, and Yayoi Kusama's mesmerizing Infinity Mirrored Rooms. Across the street stands Frank Gehry's architectural masterpiece, the stainless-steel Walt Disney Concert Hall.

Authentic Culinary Corridors: Beyond Tourist Dining

Los Angeles is recognized by culinary authorities as one of the most dynamic, culturally diverse food capitals in the world. Bypassing overpriced tourist strip restaurants in favor of authentic cultural corridors transforms a vacation into a memorable culinary journey:

1. Grand Central Market (Downtown LA)

Operating continuously since 1917 on South Broadway, Grand Central Market houses dozens of independent food vendors beneath vintage neon signs. Visitors can savor artisanal egg sandwiches at Eggslut, authentic Michoacan-style carnitas at Tacos Tumbras a Tomas, traditional Salvadoran pupusas at Sarita's Pupuseria, and handcrafted smoked pastrami at Wexler's Deli.

2. Koreatown (K-Town) 24-Hour Dining

Spanning nearly three square miles west of Downtown, Koreatown offers the highest concentration of late-night dining in California. Food lovers flock to historic barbecue institutions like Parks BBQ and Kang Ho-dong Baekjeong for prime short rib cooked over tabletop grills, alongside bubbling cauldrons of spicy soft tofu stew at BCD Tofu House.

3. San Gabriel Valley (SGV) Chinese Enclave

Located just 15 minutes east of downtown, the San Gabriel Valley is North America's premier destination for regional Chinese cuisine. Cities like Monterey Park, Alhambra, and Arcadia boast world-renowned soup dumplings (xiao long bao) at Din Tai Fung, hand-pulled Shaanxi biang biang noodles, authentic Sichuan hot pot, and artisanal boba teahouses.

4. Sawtelle Japantown and Little Tokyo

On the Westside, Sawtelle Boulevard (historically known as Little Osaka) is celebrated for its dense corridor of ramen shops, including the rich dipping broth at Tsujita LA Artisan Noodle. On the east side, historic Little Tokyo (established in 1884) features century-old mochi bakeries like Fugetsu-Do and traditional tonkotsu ramen at Daikokuya.

5. East LA Street Taco Culture

No trip to Southern California is complete without tasting authentic Mexican street gastronomy. Operating out of legendary food trucks along Olympic Boulevard, Mariscos Jalisco serves world-famous tacos dorados de camaron (crispy fried shrimp tacos topped with fresh avocado and spicy tomato salsa), celebrated by culinary critics worldwide.

Hidden Gems and Non-Touristy Los Angeles Treasures

For travelers seeking unique, off-the-beaten-path experiences far away from crowded tourist buses, Los Angeles hides extraordinary cultural sanctuaries:

The Museum of Jurassic Technology (Culver City)

Tucked behind an unassuming storefront in Culver City, this labyrinthine, dimly lit museum is an eccentric cabinet of curiosities blending real science, artistic wonder, and whimsical fabrication. Exhibits include microminiature sculptures carved into the eyes of needles, antique scientific oddities, and a tranquil rooftop tea room inhabited by live doves.

Watts Towers by Simon Rodia

Constructed single-handedly by Italian immigrant Simon Rodia over a span of 33 years (1921 to 1954), the Watts Towers consist of 17 interconnected sculptural spires rising nearly 100 feet into the air. Built using hand tools, structural steel, mortar, and mosaic decorations crafted from sea shells, broken bottles, porcelain tiles, and mirrors, the towers stand as a National Historic Landmark and an extraordinary masterpiece of folk art.

The Huntington Library and Botanical Gardens (San Marino)

Covering 130 acres in the residential city of San Marino, The Huntington combines an exceptional research library holding rare treasures like a Gutenberg Bible and Shakespeare first folios with 16 world-renowned themed botanical gardens. Highlights include the classical Chinese Garden (Liu Fang Yuan), one of the largest Suzhou-style gardens outside of China, and the historic Japanese Garden featuring a moon bridge and bonsai pavilion.

Self-Realization Fellowship Lake Shrine (Pacific Palisades)

Hidden just moments from the Pacific Coast Highway in Pacific Palisades, the Lake Shrine is a serene 10-acre public garden sanctuary surrounding a spring-fed natural lake. Established in 1950 by Paramahansa Yogananda, the grounds feature walking paths, floating swans, waterfalls, and the Mahatma Gandhi World Peace Memorial, which holds a portion of Gandhi's ashes in a thousand-year-old Chinese sarcophagus.

Coastal Escapes: Santa Monica, Venice, and Malibu

Los Angeles boasts dozens of miles of pristine Pacific coastline, each beach town displaying its own distinct atmosphere:

Santa Monica Pier and State Beach

Marking the official western terminus of historic Route 66, the Santa Monica Pier features Pacific Park, home to the world's only solar-powered Ferris wheel, a vintage 1922 Looff Hippodrome carousel, and casual seaside seafood eateries. Travelers can stroll down the pedestrianized Third Street Promenade for retail shopping and street performances.

Venice Beach Boardwalk and Canals

Just two miles south of Santa Monica lies Venice, established in 1905 by tobacco millionaire Abbot Kinney as a seaside homage to Venice, Italy. While the Ocean Front Walk buzzes with eccentric street buskers, mural artists, the public skate park, and the historic Muscle Beach outdoor gymnasium, the serene Venice Canals located just three blocks inland offer arched pedestrian bridges, water reflections, and mid-century craftsman bungalows.

Malibu and the Pacific Coast Highway (PCH)

Driving north along California State Route 1 (PCH) brings travelers to the dramatic coastal cliffs of Malibu. Notable coastal stops include El Matador State Beach with its sea caves and natural rock arches, Point Dume for seasonal whale watching from oceanfront bluffs, and the Malibu Pier for fresh farm-to-table coastal dining.

Outdoor Hikes, Mountain Canyons, and Secret Stairs

Despite its reputation as an asphalt jungle, Los Angeles is bifurcated by the rugged Santa Monica Mountain range, creating extraordinary urban wilderness trails:

Runyon Canyon Park (Hollywood)

Located right above Hollywood Boulevard, Runyon Canyon offers a 2.7-mile loop trail famous for panoramic views of the Los Angeles skyline, celebrity sightings, and off-leash dog paths. The Cloud's Rest viewpoint at the top provides sweeping vistas from downtown to the Pacific Ocean.

Secret Hillside Staircases of Silver Lake

Built in the 1920s to connect steep hillside residential neighborhoods with historic streetcar lines, LA's secret staircases provide a unique walking adventure. The Micheltorena Stairs (decorated with painted hearts and rainbows) and the Swan Stairs in Silver Lake offer a steep, scenic cardiovascular workout past historic modernist architecture.

Topanga State Park and Parker Mesa Overlook

Located entirely within the city limits of Los Angeles, Topanga State Park is recognized as the largest wildland park surrounded by a major metropolis in the world. The trail to Parker Mesa Overlook winds through coastal sage scrub to a blufftop view looking out over Santa Monica Bay and Catalina Island.

Best Free Things to Do in Los Angeles

Sightseeing in a premier global destination does not require an immense budget. Los Angeles provides numerous zero-cost activities:

  • Free Cultural Museums: The Getty Center, Getty Villa in Pacific Palisades, The Broad, and the California Science Center offer completely free general admission with advance registration.
  • Scenic Hiking Trails: Hike Runyon Canyon for celebrity sightings and Hollywood views, explore the shaded canyon trails of Will Rogers State Historic Park, or trek to the secluded Murphy Ranch ruins in Pacific Palisades.
  • Architectural Landmarks: Walk through the historic Bradbury Building with its ironwork atrium, visit the Spanish-Revival Union Station, and explore the birthplace of the city at Olvera Street in El Pueblo de Los Angeles.
  • Public Gardens and Parks: Stroll through the Japanese Garden at the Donald C. Tillman Water Reclamation Plant in Van Nuys or relax beside the lotus beds at Echo Park Lake.

Getting Around, New Subway Lines, and Transit Tips

Navigating Southern California successfully requires setting realistic transit expectations and understanding local geographical patterns:

The 2026 Transit Revolution: For decades, visitors were told that exploring Los Angeles without a car was impossible. That narrative changed dramatically with the opening of the Metro D Line (Purple Line) Extension Section 1, which adds underground subway stations at Wilshire/La Brea, Wilshire/Fairfax, and Wilshire/La Cienega. Travelers can now ride direct from Downtown LA Union Station directly to Museum Row (LACMA, Academy Museum, and Petersen Museum) in just 21 minutes, bypassing Wilshire Boulevard traffic entirely. Combined with the E Line running to Santa Monica beach, smart travelers can explore top sights without dealing with parking garages.

Car Rental & Road Trip Essentials: Renting a vehicle remains the most versatile option for exploring varied destinations from Malibu to the San Gabriel Valley. Drivers should familiarize themselves with vehicle essentials like how to change a flat tire before embarking on scenic road trips along the Pacific Coast Highway or mountain canyon routes.

Where to Anchor Your Stay: If ocean breezes and bike paths are your top priority, base your stay in Santa Monica or Venice. For visitors focused on central sightseeing, dining, and nightlife, West Hollywood or Beverly Hills provides convenient central positioning. Travelers seeking culinary variety and historic architecture will appreciate Downtown Los Angeles or the Arts District.

Navigating Rush Hour: Traffic on Interstate 405, Highway 101, and Interstate 10 reaches peak density on weekdays between 7:00 AM and 10:00 AM in the morning and 4:00 PM and 7:00 PM in the evening. Scheduling museum visits, dining experiences, or beach walks during these time windows keeps you off the road when highways are slowest.

Frequently Asked Questions About Visiting Los Angeles

What are the top must-see attractions in Los Angeles for first-time visitors?

First-time visitors should prioritize Griffith Observatory for Hollywood Sign and skyline views, an authentic studio tour at Warner Bros. or Universal Studios, the art collections at The Getty Center, Santa Monica Pier, Venice Beach Boardwalk, and the historic cultural landmarks of Downtown Los Angeles like The Broad and Grand Central Market.

How many days do you need to visit Los Angeles?

A well-planned trip requires at least 4 to 5 full days. Because Los Angeles spans over 500 square miles across 80 distinct neighborhoods, grouping attractions by geographic zones (Westside beaches, Central Hollywood, Downtown LA, and The Valley) allows you to explore thoroughly without spending hours stuck in freeway traffic.

What are the best free things to do in Los Angeles?

Top free activities include visiting Griffith Observatory and its grounds, exploring The Getty Center and Getty Villa (advance timed reservation required, parking fee applies), visiting The Broad contemporary art museum, walking the historic Venice Beach Canals, touring the Bradbury Building, and hiking Runyon Canyon.

Do you need a car to visit Los Angeles in 2026?

While renting a car offers flexibility for day trips to Malibu or the canyons, visiting LA without a car is now easier than ever. The newly opened D Line subway extension connects Downtown LA directly to Museum Row on Wilshire Boulevard in 21 minutes, while the E Line connects directly to Santa Monica beach.

Which studio tour is better: Universal Studios or Warner Bros?

Universal Studios Hollywood is a full-scale theme park featuring thrill rides, Super Nintendo World, and a classic 60-minute studio tram tour. Warner Bros. Studio Tour Hollywood is an authentic, guided behind-the-scenes walking and cart tour focused on real working soundstages, television sets, and prop archives for dedicated film buffs.

Where is the best place to view the Hollywood Sign?

The easiest panoramic view is from the lawn of Griffith Observatory. For closer angles without disturbing residential neighbors, visit Lake Hollywood Park or hike the Mt. Hollywood Trail from Griffith Park. Avoid driving into narrow residential hills where parking is strictly restricted.

What is the best time of year to visit Los Angeles?

The ideal visiting window runs from March to May (spring) and September to November (autumn). During these months, temperatures are pleasant (70 to 80 degrees Fahrenheit), crowds are lighter than peak summer, and you avoid June Gloom morning coastal fog.

What are the best things to do in Los Angeles with kids?

Families enjoy Universal Studios Hollywood, interactive science exhibits at the California Science Center, riding the solar-powered Ferris wheel at Santa Monica Pier, viewing prehistoric fossils at the La Brea Tar Pits, and visiting the sea life exhibits at the Aquarium of the Pacific in nearby Long Beach.

How do you avoid traffic when sightseeing in Los Angeles?

The most effective strategy is geographic clustering: dedicate each day entirely to one quadrant of the city (for example, Westside beaches on Monday, Downtown on Tuesday). Avoid driving on major freeways like the 405 and 101 during morning rush hour (7:00 AM to 10:00 AM) and evening rush hour (4:00 PM to 7:00 PM).

What are the best hidden gems in Los Angeles that tourists miss?

Top non-touristy gems include The Museum of Jurassic Technology in Culver City, the historic Watts Towers, the peaceful botanical grounds of the Huntington Library in San Marino, the Self-Realization Fellowship Lake Shrine in Pacific Palisades, and the secret hillside staircases of Silver Lake.
