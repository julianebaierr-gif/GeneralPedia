Editorial Standards

Editorial Guidelines, Fact-Checking & Sourcing Policy

The foundational principles that guide every article, calculator, data table, and analytical breakdown published across GeneralPedia.com.

1. The GeneralPedia Editorial Commitment

At GeneralPedia.com, we believe that factual accuracy is the bedrock of public trust. Millions of global internet users rely on digital encyclopedias to make consequential life decisions, ranging from managing hard-earned personal savings to interpreting medical symptoms and buying family vehicles.

Our editorial staff, freelance contributors, and data researchers adhere to strict editorial ethics, uncompromising fact-checking protocols, and transparent attribution practices. We measure our success not by sensational headlines, but by the depth, clarity, and factual durability of our publications.

2. Four-Step Fact-Checking & Verification Lifecycle

Before an article or interactive module is published on GeneralPedia, it must successfully navigate our structured four-tier editorial workflow:

1

Primary Source Research

Authors must extract data directly from authoritative primary sources: statutory legislative records (IRS, Congressional Budget Office), peer-reviewed scientific journals (PubMed, Nature, The Lancet), or recognized engineering standards (SAE, ISO). Secondhand hearsay or unverified forum commentary is strictly rejected.

2

Subject-Matter Editorial Review

Senior section editors—such as Marcus Reid (How-To), Sarah Mitchell (Finance), Elena Torres (Health), and David Chen (Automotive)—cross-examine all statistical claims, formulas, and legal thresholds against active clinical consensus and statutory fiscal calendars.

3

Clarity & Readability Optimization

Technical terminology is clearly defined. Content is structured with clear bullet points, comparison tables, bold highlights, and direct answers to reader intent, avoiding unnecessary fluff.

4

Post-Publication Auditing & Freshness Updates

Content is not static. Our editors continuously monitor regulatory changes, tax bracket annual adjustments, interest rate shifts, and vehicle recall alerts, updating existing articles promptly.

3. Citation & Sourcing Hierarchy

To guarantee absolute transparency, our writers cite reputable resources in accordance with the following hierarchy:

  • Tier 1 (Governmental & Regulatory Repositories): IRS.gov, CDC.gov, FDA.gov, SEC.gov, BLS.gov, DOT.gov, NIH.gov.
  • Tier 2 (Academic & Scientific Institutions): Stanford, Harvard, MIT, Nature, JSTOR, PubMed, ScienceDirect.
  • Tier 3 (Accredited Non-Profit Organizations & Standards Bodies): American Heart Association, Brookings Institution, SAE International.
  • Tier 4 (Recognized Industry Publications): Bloomberg, Reuters, Edmunds, Kelley Blue Book.

We strictly forbid linking to content farms, low-quality aggregators, sponsored spam blogs, or biased promotional advertorials.

4. Policy on Artificial Intelligence (AI) & Automation

At GeneralPedia, we embrace technological innovation while strictly upholding human editorial oversight. We align fully with Google's Search Central guidelines regarding AI-generated content:

Human-in-the-Loop Requirement

While algorithmic tools and LLMs may assist in data aggregation, semantic clustering, or stylistic proofreading, no article is ever published without comprehensive human review, factual verification, and editorial refinement.

5. Corrections & Feedback Protocol

Despite our exhaustive vetting protocols, errors or outdated regulatory figures may occasionally occur. GeneralPedia is committed to swift, transparent corrections. If you detect a factual discrepancy or outdated data point in any article:

Report a Factual Inaccuracy

Send the article URL and supporting documentation to our editorial team.
