Geographic Location and Regional Boundaries of Area Code 323

The 323 area code is a major North American Numbering Plan (NANP) telephone code serving the urban core, creative hubs, and industrial gateways of Los Angeles County, California. Formed in 1998 and completely overlaid with the historic 213 area code in 2017, the 323 territory encompasses iconic neighborhoods including Hollywood, Silver Lake, Los Feliz, Koreatown, South Los Angeles, and East Los Angeles, alongside adjacent municipalities like Huntington Park and South Gate.

Operating entirely within the Pacific Time Zone, area code 323 represents one of the most culturally dense, economically vibrant, and telephonically active regions in the United States. Whether researching an unknown incoming call, establishing business operations in Central Los Angeles, or reviewing dialing requirements, this guide provides an exhaustive directory of municipalities, carrier routing structures, and fraud verification protocols.

Time Zone and Daylight Saving Schedule

Area code 323 is situated entirely inside the Pacific Time Zone (PT) of the United States. It observes seasonal daylight saving transitions mandated by the Uniform Time Act:

  • Pacific Standard Time (PST): Observed from the first Sunday in November until the second Sunday in March. During this period, local time is UTC-8 (Coordinated Universal Time minus 8 hours).
  • Pacific Daylight Time (PDT): Observed from the second Sunday in March until the first Sunday in November. Clocks advance one hour forward, shifting local time to UTC-7.

Because Los Angeles serves as a premier global hub for international trade, entertainment production, and financial technology, maintaining exact UTC time synchronization is essential when scheduling cross-country business transactions with Eastern (UTC-5), Central (UTC-6), or Mountain (UTC-7) counterparts.

Historical Evolution: From the 1998 Boundary Split to the 2017 All-Services Overlay

The telecommunications geography of Southern California underwent major structural shifts over the past century to manage unprecedented demand for telephone numbers:

1. The Original 1947 Footprint

When AT&T and the Bell System established the North American Numbering Plan in October 1947, area code 213 was assigned as one of the original 86 area codes, blanketing the entirety of Southern California from the Pacific coastline inland to Nevada and south to Mexico.

2. The June 13, 1998 Geographic Split

The late 1990s witnessed an explosive proliferation of secondary fax lines, pagers, commercial dial-up internet modems, and consumer cellular phones. With 213 rapidly exhausting its 7.92 million central office prefix capacity, the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) approved a geographic split on June 13, 1998.

Under this division, downtown Los Angeles retained the prestigious 213 designation across roughly 15 square miles. The surrounding territory—spanning over 180 square miles across Hollywood, South Los Angeles, and East LA—was detached and assigned the new 323 area code. Following a six-month permissive dialing window, callers outside downtown were required to use 323.

3. CPUC Decision 16-07-014 and the 2017 Overlay

By 2015, numbering forecasts revealed that area code 323 was again nearing exhaustion, with available prefixes projected to deplete within two years. Simultaneously, area code 213 in downtown possessed unused number blocks.

Rather than implementing another disruptive geographic boundary carving, the CPUC issued Decision 16-07-014 on July 14, 2016, approving an all-services geographic overlay. Effective August 1, 2017, the border separating 213 and 323 was permanently abolished. Both area codes now serve the exact same combined geographic footprint. Existing customers maintained their numbers, while newly activated phone lines can be assigned either a 213 or 323 prefix anywhere across the metropolitan territory.

Dialing Instructions and the Mandatory 10-Digit Rule

Because the 213 and 323 codes coexist across identical streets and neighborhoods, legacy 7-digit local dialing is strictly prohibited. The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) and CPUC mandate 10-digit dialing for all telephone traffic originated within the overlay zone:

Local Calls: Dial Area Code + 7-Digit Number (e.g., 323-555-0199)
Long-Distance / Toll Calls: Dial 1 + Area Code + 7-Digit Number (e.g., 1-323-555-0199)

Attempting to dial only seven digits will trigger a carrier intercept message informing the caller that ten digits are required. Automatic dialing systems, PBX commercial switchboards, medical alert monitors, security alarm panels, and speed-dial contacts must be programmed with full 10-digit formats.

Adjacent Area Codes Across Greater Los Angeles

Area code 323 is bordered by five neighboring Southern California numbering plan areas:

  • 213 (Downtown Los Angeles): Direct geographic overlay sharing the identical territory, centered on the historic financial and civic core.
  • 310 / 424 (Westside & South Bay): Directly borders 323 to the west and southwest, covering Beverly Hills, Santa Monica, Culver City, Century City, Torrance, and the South Bay beach communities.
  • 818 / 747 (San Fernando Valley): Borders 323 to the north, covering Burbank, Glendale, Pasadena, North Hollywood, Van Nuys, and Woodland Hills.
  • 626 (San Gabriel Valley): Borders 323 to the east, encompassing Alhambra, Pasadena, Arcadia, El Monte, and West Covina.
  • 562 (Long Beach & Gateway Cities): Borders 323 to the southeast, covering Long Beach, Whittier, Downey, Norwalk, and Lakewood.

Reverse Phone Lookup, Spam Defense & Number Verification

Because area code 323 houses major entertainment hubs and financial districts, it is frequently targeted by illicit telemarketers using neighbor spoofing—manipulating caller ID equipment to display local 323 prefixes to trick recipients into answering. Understanding how to investigate unfamiliar numbers protects consumers against sophisticated fraud:

1. Identifying Common 323 Impersonation Vectors

  • Entertainment Casting Scams: Fraudsters claim to represent Hollywood talent agencies, requiring upfront 'audition processing fees' or deposit advances.
  • Government and Tax Impersonators: Automated robocalls pretending to be the IRS or Los Angeles County Superior Court threatening immediate legal arrest for unpaid administrative balances.
  • Utility Disconnection Frauds: High-pressure callers spoofing the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power (LADWP) claiming power will be shut off within 30 minutes unless prepaid debit card payments are executed.

2. Technical Number Authentication via STIR/SHAKEN

Modern telecommunications carriers implement the STIR/SHAKEN protocol (Secure Telephone Identity Revisited / Signature-based Handling of Asserted information using toKENs). When a call originates from a verified network, the originating provider cryptographically signs the call with an attestation rating:

  • Full Attestation (Grade A): The provider verifies the calling customer identity and confirms they have the legal right to use the specific 323 telephone number. Modern smartphones display a 'Verified Caller' icon or checkmark.
  • Partial Attestation (Grade B): The provider verifies the customer originating the call but cannot verify their authorization to use the telephone number.
  • Gateway Attestation (Grade C): The call entered the network from an international gateway or third-party wholesale bridge with zero origin verification—the primary hallmark of spoofed robocall spam.

3. Actionable Consumer Protection Steps

If you receive persistent unsolicited calls from unfamiliar 323 numbers, register your residential and mobile lines on the National Do Not Call Registry (donotcall.gov). In addition, activate carrier-level screening applications (such as Verizon Call Filter, AT&T ActiveArmor, or T-Mobile Scam Shield) to automatically block unauthorized Grade C VoIP calls before your handset rings.
