2026 Winter Olympics: Location, Schedule, Sports & Updates

Review the 2026 Winter Olympics schedule, Milano Cortina venues, new sports, ticket logistics, and full competition dates across northern Italy.

Amir Hassan
September 15, 2026
6 min read

The 2026 Winter Olympics, officially designated as the XXV Olympic Winter Games, will take place across northern Italy from February 6 through February 22, 2026. Jointly hosted by the global fashion metropolis of Milan and the legendary alpine resort of Cortina d'Ampezzo, the Games introduce a pioneering decentralized model that stretches across twenty-two thousand square kilometers of dramatic mountain landscapes and urban centers.

Marking Italy's fourth Olympic celebration following Cortina 1956, Rome 1960, and Turin 2006, Milano Cortina 2026 represents a structural shift for the International Olympic Committee (IOC). Built upon the principles of the Olympic Agenda 2020 "New Norm," over ninety percent of competition venues leverage existing sports infrastructure or temporary installations, making this edition a blueprint for modern sustainability and regional cooperation.

Key Takeaways & Event Overview

  • The XXV Olympic Winter Games run from February 6 to February 22, 2026, followed by the Winter Paralympics from March 6 to March 15, 2026.
  • Competitions are organized across four geographic clusters in northern Italy: Milan (Ice Sports), Valtellina (Men's Alpine & Snowboarding), Val di Fiemme (Nordic Events), and Cortina d'Ampezzo (Women's Alpine, Curling & Sliding).
  • The Opening Ceremony takes place at the iconic San Siro Stadium in Milan, while the Closing Ceremony will be staged inside the historic Roman Arena di Verona amphitheater.
  • Ski mountaineering makes its historic Olympic debut with three medal events (men's sprint, women's sprint, mixed relay).
  • Official tickets are distributed through a centralized digital platform utilizing randomized time-slot registration phases to prevent unauthorized scalping.

Geographical Clusters & Venue Distribution

Unlike traditional Olympic Games confined to a single host city with nearby slopes, Milano Cortina 2026 spans two autonomous provinces (Trento and Bolzano/South Tyrol) and two administrative regions (Lombardy and Veneto). To manage operations and spectator movement, organizers divided the event footprint into four distinct operational clusters:

  • Milan Cluster (Metropolitan Ice Sports): Serves as the primary urban hub. The Mediolanum Forum in Assago hosts figure skating and short track speed skating. Men's and women's ice hockey are divided between the newly constructed PalaItalia Santa Giulia arena in southeastern Milan and the temporary Fiera Milano exhibition pavilion. The historic San Siro (Stadio Giuseppe Meazza) will host more than seventy-five thousand spectators for the grand Opening Ceremony.
  • Cortina d'Ampezzo Cluster (The Dolomites Hub): Centered in the dramatic Ampezzo valley. The legendary Olimpia delle Tofane slope hosts women's alpine skiing speed events (Downhill and Super-G). Curling takes place in the renovated Stadio Olimpico del Ghiaccio, the original centerpiece of the 1956 Winter Games. Bobsleigh, skeleton, and luge are scheduled for the modernized Eugenio Monti sliding track.
  • Valtellina Cluster (Extreme Alpine & Snow Sports): Situated along the Swiss border in Lombardy. The legendary Stelvio slope in Bormio, renowned as one of the steepest and most demanding downhill tracks on the World Cup circuit, hosts men's alpine skiing and the historic debut of ski mountaineering. Nearby Livigno, known for world-class snow parks, hosts all freestyle skiing and snowboarding competitions across Mottolino and Carosello 3000.
  • Val di Fiemme & Antholz Cluster (Nordic & Biathlon): Predazzo and Lago di Tesero in the Trentino Dolomites host ski jumping, cross-country skiing, and Nordic combined. In neighboring South Tyrol, the world-famous Südtirol Arena in Antholz (Anterselva) welcomes biathlon competitions at an altitude of sixteen hundred meters.

Master Competition Calendar & Milestone Dates

Across seventeen days of elite athletic competition, nearly three thousand athletes representing over ninety National Olympic Committees will contest one hundred and sixteen medal events in sixteen Olympic disciplines:

  • February 4 to 5, 2026 (Preliminary Rounds): Action commences two days prior to the official opening ceremony with preliminary round matches in mixed doubles curling in Cortina and opening group stage games in women's ice hockey in Milan.
  • February 6, 2026 (Opening Ceremony): Official opening celebration at San Siro Stadium in Milan, featuring the Parade of Nations, artistic tributes to Italian heritage, and the lighting of the Olympic cauldron.
  • February 7 to 8, 2026 (Opening Medal Weekend): The first gold medals are awarded in men's downhill on the icy Stelvio course in Bormio, speed skating at Fiera Milano, women's skiathlon in Val di Fiemme, and the biathlon mixed relay in Antholz.
  • February 14 to 15, 2026 (Mid-Games Peak): High-profile weekend showcasing the figure skating ice dance free dance finals, women's downhill on Cortina's Tofane, men's snowboard big air in Livigno, and the conclusion of the curling mixed doubles tournament.
  • February 19 to 21, 2026 (Ski Mountaineering & Hockey Semifinals): Debut of ski mountaineering sprint competitions in Bormio, alongside the high-stakes semifinals of men's and women's ice hockey featuring top NHL professionals.
  • February 22, 2026 (Super Sunday & Closing Ceremony): The final day features the men's ice hockey gold medal game and the traditional women's 50km cross-country mass start. In the evening, the Games culminate inside the ancient Roman amphitheater Arena di Verona with the formal handover to the French Alps 2030 organizers.

New Sports & Medal Event Additions

The Milano Cortina program introduces strategic additions designed to modernize winter sport engagement and celebrate alpine traditions:

  • Ski Mountaineering (Skimo Debut): Making its Olympic debut following successful testing at the 2020 Winter Youth Olympic Games in Lausanne. Athletes climb steep alpine ascents using climbing skins on lightweight skis, travel across rocky boot-packing sections with skis strapped to backpacks, and race down high-speed off-piste descents. Three medal events are contested: men's sprint, women's sprint, and mixed team relay.
  • Dual Moguls Freestyle Skiing: Transitioning from traditional solo mogul runs to head-to-head knockout racing, where two skiers race down parallel mogul courses simultaneously, evaluated on speed, turn quality, and aerial acrobatics.
  • Women's Individual Large Hill Ski Jumping: Expands gender parity in ski jumping, granting women equal medal opportunities on the large hill alongside the normal hill competition.
  • Skeleton Mixed Team Event: Pairs one male and one female slider per nation, with combined aggregate run times determining the medal standings.
  • Team Alpine Combined: Replaces individual alpine combined, featuring one downhill specialist and one slalom specialist teaming up to represent their national federation.

Ticketing Logistics & Purchasing Protocols

Ticketing for the 2026 Games follows a transparent, digital-only model orchestrated by the Milano Cortina organizing committee to prevent unauthorized markups:

  • Phased Ballot Registration: Prospective attendees register on the official ticketing portal to participate in randomized lottery draws. Winners receive dedicated forty-eight-hour windows to select and purchase their desired sessions.
  • General Public Release: Unsold tickets and returns are released on a first-come, first-served basis following the completion of the ballot phases.
  • Pricing Tiers: Organizers established accessible entry pricing, with over twenty percent of all tickets priced below forty euros and more than half priced below one hundred euros. Premium sessions, including the ice hockey gold medal game and Opening Ceremony, command higher tiered pricing.
  • Official Hospitality: Premium ticket packages bundled with guaranteed four-star and five-star accommodations, gourmet dining, and private transfers are managed exclusively through the IOC's official hospitality partner, On Location.

The Cortina Sliding Centre Resolution

The most scrutinized venue project of the 2026 cycle was the Cortina Sliding Centre. The historic Eugenio Monti track, which hosted the 1956 Olympic bobsleigh events, had fallen into disuse in 2008. The IOC originally urged Italian organizers to move sliding events abroad to existing tracks in Igls (Austria) or St. Moritz (Switzerland) to avoid costly construction.

However, the Italian government and Veneto regional authorities committed eighty-one million euros to rebuild the track on its historic footprint in Cortina. Despite tight construction schedules, detailed refrigeration tests and track homologation trials ensure the facility meets international bobsleigh, luge, and skeleton federation standards while preserving Italy's century-old sliding heritage.

Global Broadcasting & Streaming Networks

Spectators worldwide can follow live coverage of the 2026 Winter Games across major media partnerships:

  • United States: NBCUniversal holds exclusive rights. Live coverage airs on the NBC broadcast network and USA Network, with every single event streamed live on Peacock in 4K HDR.
  • Canada: CBC/Radio-Canada delivers detailed coverage across linear television, the CBC Gem streaming platform, and the CBC Sports app with English and French commentary.
  • United Kingdom: Discovery+ and Eurosport provide wall-to-wall live streaming of every competition, complemented by free-to-air coverage on BBC One and BBC iPlayer.
  • Europe: Warner Bros. Discovery and the European Broadcasting Union (EBU) distribute multi-language feeds across fifty European territories. In Italy, public broadcaster RAI provides free-to-air domestic coverage.
  • Australia: Nine Network and Stan Sport deliver exclusive round-the-clock winter Olympic broadcasting and streaming.

Spectator Travel Logistics & Alpine Transport

Navigating the mountainous terrain of northern Italy requires advance logistical planning due to distances between clusters:

  • Milan Connections: Served by Milan Malpensa, Linate, and Bergamo airports, interconnected by high-speed rail networks (Frecciarossa and Italo) that connect to Venice, Verona, and Rome within two to three hours.
  • Reaching Cortina d'Ampezzo: Cortina does not have a direct train station. Spectators travel by train to Calalzo di Cadore or Venice Mestre, followed by dedicated Olympic express shuttle coaches taking approximately ninety minutes to two hours.
  • Valtellina Access (Bormio & Livigno): Regional trains connect Milan Central to Tirano in two and a half hours, where coordinated Olympic shuttle buses transfer spectators through the valley to Bormio and Livigno.
  • Winter Driving Regulations: From November 15 to April 15, Italian law mandates that all passenger vehicles in the Alps be equipped with certified winter snow tires or carry approved snow chains. High alpine passes (including the Stelvio Pass) are closed during winter, funneling road traffic through lower valley corridors.

Analyzing the long-term infrastructural legacy of the Milano Cortina Games demonstrates how northern Italy balances historic preservation with modern athletic requirements. Municipal authorities in Milan prioritized the urban renewal of the Santa Giulia district through the construction of the PalaItalia arena, which will transition into a permanent multipurpose entertainment venue following the Paralympic Closing Ceremony. In the mountain valleys, investments focused on upgrading existing ski lifts, expanding high-capacity regional bus corridors, and modernizing avalanche safety monitoring networks rather than building redundant megastructures.

Weather resilience and snowmaking guarantees represent another pillar of the organizing committee's technical planning. Leveraging the cutting-edge snowmaking infrastructure of the Dolomiti Superski resort network, venue managers installed energy-efficient automated snow cannons connected to high-altitude water storage reservoirs. These measures guarantee consistent, regulation-grade snowpack across the downhill runs of Cortina and the steep slopes of Bormio even during unseasonably mild winter periods.

The 2026 Winter Paralympic Games

Following the Olympic Closing Ceremony, northern Italy welcomes the XIV Paralympic Winter Games from March 6 to March 15, 2026. Over six hundred para-athletes will compete in seventy-nine medal events across six core disciplines:

  • Para Alpine Skiing & Para Snowboard: Staged across the slopes of Cortina d'Ampezzo and Livigno.
  • Para Ice Hockey: Hosted at the newly built PalaItalia arena in Milan.
  • Wheelchair Curling: Contested at the Cortina Olympic Stadium.
  • Para Nordic (Biathlon & Cross-Country): Hosted in Val di Fiemme.

The Paralympic Games utilize the ancient Roman Arena di Verona for their Opening Ceremony, emphasizing barrier-free accessibility modifications that leave a lasting civil legacy throughout northern Italy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the exact dates for the 2026 Winter Olympics?

The 2026 Winter Olympics take place from February 6 to February 22, 2026. Preliminary competition rounds begin two days early on February 4, and the Winter Paralympic Games follow from March 6 to March 15, 2026.

Which cities and venues are hosting the 2026 Winter Olympics?

The Games are co-hosted by Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo, with events spread across four regional clusters: Milan (ice sports), Cortina d'Ampezzo (women's alpine skiing, curling, sledding), Valtellina (men's alpine skiing in Bormio, snowboarding and freestyle in Livigno), and Val di Fiemme/Antholz (Nordic events and biathlon).

Where will the Opening and Closing Ceremonies be held?

The Opening Ceremony will be held on February 6, 2026, at the San Siro Stadium in Milan. The Closing Ceremony will take place on February 22, 2026, inside the ancient Roman Arena di Verona amphitheater.

What new sport makes its Olympic debut in 2026?

Ski mountaineering (Skimo) makes its official Olympic debut at the 2026 Winter Games, featuring three medal events: men's sprint, women's sprint, and a mixed team relay contested in Bormio.

How can I purchase official tickets for Milano Cortina 2026?

Official tickets are sold exclusively through the official digital ticketing platform of the Milano Cortina organizing committee. Fans must register for randomized draw phases, with remaining tickets released for general public purchase afterward.

Will NHL hockey players participate in the 2026 Winter Olympics?

Yes, the International Olympic Committee and the National Hockey League confirmed an agreement allowing NHL players to represent their national teams in men's ice hockey at the 2026 Games for the first time since 2014.

How do spectators travel between Milan and the mountain venues?

Spectators can use high-speed trains to travel between major hubs like Milan, Venice, and Verona, supplemented by dedicated Olympic shuttle buses connecting rail stations to mountain valleys in Cortina, Bormio, and Val di Fiemme.

What are the mascots for the 2026 Winter Olympic Games?

The official mascots are Tina and Milo, two stoat siblings. Tina represents the Olympic Games and symbolizes curiosity and creativity, while Milo, who was born without a leg, represents the Paralympic Games and embodies resilience and determination.

Are the 2026 Winter Olympics environmentally sustainable?

Yes, Milano Cortina 2026 operates under the IOC New Norm framework, using more than ninety percent existing or temporary venues, relying on renewable energy sources, and improving public transport infrastructure to minimize environmental impact.
